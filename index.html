Abstract

Abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models across robot embodiments require high-quality observation–action supervision to learn deployable action distributions, yet scaling such robot data remains difficult, especially for high-DoF humanoids. Teleoperation provides controller-aligned supervision, while human egocentric videos capture diverse bimanual manipulation but do not directly provide executable robot actions. We introduce Human-as-Humanoid, a human-to-humanoid supervision framework that enables near-real-time human-centric action generation, making human demonstrations usable for high-DoF humanoid VLA training by jointly aligning the robot embodiment, the sensing setup, and the action-label interface. Built on PrimeU, a human-aligned 60-DoF upper-body humanoid, Human-as-Humanoid uses synchronized ego-exo videos to pair deployment-aligned egocentric observations with exocentric motion recovery, retargets the recovered human motion through staged Inverse Kinematics (IK) into controller-aligned 60-DoF action chunks, and trains the VLA model with Forward Kinematics (FK)-aware supervision to preserve wrist and fingertip task-space geometry. This converts large-scale human demonstrations from visual observations into executable observation–action supervision for the target humanoid. Experiments validate the conversion chain at the motion-recovery, robot-action-space, and real-robot deployment levels. Human-as-Humanoid yields a 4.8–7.2× raw demonstration-throughput gain over humanoid teleoperation in our data-collection analysis, and on several downstream tasks, policies post-trained only with the converted human labels generalize to real-robot deployment without target-task robot demonstrations.

Why It's Hard

Four Coupled Requirements

Converting human video into robot-executable supervision is not just "collect more video." It exposes four coupled requirements that Human-as-Humanoid addresses jointly.

i

Embodiment Alignment

Robot morphology and sensing layout stay compatible with human demonstrations, reducing retargeting error from body scale, workspace, and viewpoint gaps.

ii

Observation–Motion Compatibility

Egocentric streams give deployment-aligned policy inputs; exocentric views give robust upper-body and hand recovery under occlusion.

iii

Action-Interface Alignment

Converted labels respect the robot's joint ordering, URDF convention, joint limits, and controller interface — not just task-space intent.

iv

Joint–Task Consistency

Executable joint commands preserve the wrist and fingertip geometry that contact-rich manipulation depends on.

Embodiment

PrimeU: A Human-Aligned Humanoid

Instead of treating human-to-robot transfer as a purely post-hoc retargeting problem, Human-as-Humanoid starts from the embodiment. PrimeU's upper body follows standard adult-male manipulation proportions — height, shoulder width, and arm length — so common human demonstrations occupy a workspace close to the robot's reachable workspace.

Method

Near-Real-Time Human-to-Humanoid Conversion

Synchronized ego-exo human videos are converted into executable humanoid action labels at collection time. Egocentric views provide deployment-aligned observations; exocentric views support robust upper-body and hand motion recovery. A staged IK solver then retargets the recovered skeleton into controller-aligned 60-DoF action chunks, and a flow-matching VLA (PhysDex) is trained with FK-aware supervision (DS-HKC) that preserves wrist and fingertip geometry.

Validation · Motion Recovery

Ego-Exo Motion Recovery vs. Wearable Mocap

Camera-only ego-exo recovery is compared against a wearable inertial motion-capture suit by projecting recovered skeletons back onto the human video. The wearable suit shows visible drift from accumulated global-position error; ego-exo recovery maintains closer alignment with the observed body and hands, without giving up the egocentric stream needed for deployment-aligned policy observations.

Validation · Action Space

Action-Interface Compatibility

Do human-derived 60-DoF action chunks land in the same action space as real PrimeU trajectories? A discrete action tokenizer trained only on human-derived actions is evaluated on held-out real-robot windows it never saw during fitting.

Validation · FK-Aware Training

DS-HKC Improves Optimization Geometry

FK-aware supervision is not just an auxiliary penalty: it couples the 60 joint dimensions through the robot kinematic chain, measuring whether induced wrist and fingertip poses stay consistent with task-space manipulation geometry.

Discussion

Limitations

Pose-estimation quality bounds downstream retargeting quality, and IK quality bounds policy quality since human-derived data inherits the robot model, joint limits, and calibration used by the retargeter. The pipeline is tied to PrimeU's specific URDF and joint convention; transferring to a new embodiment requires re-retargeting. Human-derived actions also capture kinematics more directly than contact forces, so robot data remains important for anchoring, evaluation, and contact-rich refinement. The zero-shot claim refers to target-task deployment without target-task robot demonstrations, not to eliminating all robot-specific modeling assumptions.
